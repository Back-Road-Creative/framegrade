## Privacy detection (faces + plates) — the `redactcam` package (`redactcam.detect`)

Root of the blur chain. `detect_regions(video_path, sample_fps, conf, plate_model_path=…)` samples the video at a fixed `sample_fps` cadence (frame-index arithmetic via `step = round(fps / sample_fps)`, no wall-clock seek) and returns one `FrameRegions` per sampled frame, each carrying `frame_idx`, `timestamp`, and `boxes` = the union of `faces` ∪ `plates` (all `(x, y, w, h)` in NATIVE pixels — same shape as `SignDetector.bbox`). Later stages (the later blur stages) consume these to build a blur mask video and alpha-composite it into the day master; this module only locates regions.

- **Detector choice.** Faces and plates both run single-class YOLO ONNX models behind one `_YoloDetector` (faces pass `min_aspect=max_aspect=0` — no aspect gate; plates set the `blur_plate_min_aspect`/`blur_plate_max_aspect` band). The YOLO face model **replaced `deface`'s CenterFace**, which could score foliage, a hood reflection, or a convex traffic mirror *above* real faces — so a clearly identifiable roadside pedestrian could fall under `blur_conf` while leaves were blurred. At the same `blur_conf` the YOLO face model catches those faces with high confidence and does not fire on those surfaces, and it runs faster than CenterFace when tiled on GPU. CenterFace stays as a bundled fallback (`deface/centerface.onnx`, ~7 MB) used only when the face model can't be resolved (a loud WARN flags the degraded path). The split `faces`/`plates` lists on `FrameRegions` are kept so callers can treat the classes differently downstream.
- **Plate model knob.** `config.drive.plate_model_path` (operator override, `~` expanded) wins when set. When it's empty, `analysis/__init__.py:resolve_plate_model` (a `redactcam.ModelSpec` adapter) fetches `plate_model_url` (default: a YOLOv11 plate ONNX on huggingface.co) **once**, SHA256-verifies it against `plate_model_sha256`, and caches it at `~/.cache/gomoveshift/models/plate_<sha12>.onnx` (the service pins redactcam's `cache_dir` to this path so an existing warm cache is never re-fetched); later runs reuse the cache with no network. A missing override, an unset/cleared URL, or any fetch/checksum failure degrades to **faces-only** with a WARN (never fatal — the blur hard gate owns the blur-required decision). The fetch is GET-only with no data egress (the one sanctioned network-allowlist widening); point `plate_model_url` at an internal mirror to avoid the public host. Do not commit the binary — it blows the diff cap and pollutes the repo. The decoder reads the YOLO single-class output `(1, 5, N)` (each anchor `cx, cy, w, h, score`, or its `(1, N, 5)` transpose; a face-pose export with extra keypoint channels still works — only `cx,cy,w,h,score` are read), filters by `conf`, un-letterboxes to native pixels, and NMS-dedupes (`plate_iou`, default 0.45). It is forgiving — an unrecognized shape yields no detections rather than a crash.
- **Face model knob.** Mirrors the plate knob: `face_model_path` override wins, else `resolve_face_model` fetches `face_model_url` (default: a YOLOv11-face ONNX on github.com) once, SHA256-verifies against `face_model_sha256`, and caches at `~/.cache/gomoveshift/models/face_<sha12>.onnx`. An unresolvable face model degrades to the **CenterFace fallback** (not faces-only — faces are the critical privacy target and must never silently vanish); clear `face_model_url` to force the fallback.
- **Decoupled face conf + temporal confirmation** (`blur_face_conf` 0.15 vs plate `blur_conf` 0.40; `blur_face_confirm_min_detections` 2; `blur_face_sample_fps` 16). `_detect_frame` takes independent face/plate thresholds. A small clear pedestrian face scores well *below* a plate's usable conf — for example a small, distant pedestrian face can score far below the shared `blur_conf` at the frames bracketing its closest approach and stay unblurred for many frames; the plate conf can't drop to compensate (it would resurface the squarish road-sign FPs the aspect band brackets). Only the tiled path surfaces it at all (whole-frame inference returns nothing on a face that small — tiling is mandatory for recall). **But conf alone can't be lowered safely**: the YOLO face model fires on foliage, bare branches against sky, stone walls, and dark under-car shadows at confidences above those of a small real face, so a flat low conf blurs all of them and multiplies the mask size with false positives — the same foliage regression the YOLO model replaced. The separable axis is **persistence, not confidence**: a real face re-detects across consecutive samples as it moves coherently (the tracker's distance-gate association links them into ONE track), while a foliage/shadow FP fires once at a spot the camera has moved past by the next sample. So a face track is blurred only once it reaches `blur_face_confirm_min_detections` associated detections (`TrackManager.confirmed_frame_boxes`), and then **retroactively** — its low-conf discovery frames before confirmation are emitted too, since the whole track life is recorded. Faces also get their own denser `blur_face_sample_fps` cadence (16 vs the plate 8) so the small face is snapped often enough to track its walk instead of freezing while it moves out of a held box (faces are cheap relative to the plate-detector bottleneck). Plates are never confirmation-gated (recall-critical, already aspect/area-gated). Vehicle **occupants** are the one case this per-face path cannot solve: an oncoming car's driver/passenger sits behind a moving windshield where the face model latches onto the wall/roof/glass reflections rather than the face. Such a driver can be nominally detectable at low confidence for a few frames, but a densified per-frame face pass lands its boxes on the wall and roof rather than the driver, and on the wrong (right-hand-drive passenger) side. So occupants are **not** chased by face detection at all; they are covered by the **vehicle-detector cabin blur** instead (a COCO vehicle box, top fraction blurred — see the cabin bullet under the timeline below). The remaining residual: a single-sample face *away* from any vehicle (a brief distant pedestrian pass) is still dropped with the FP flicker, behind the human visual sign-off gate.
- **Reduced-res inference + rescale.** The face and plate YOLO models both letterbox to a fixed square `plate_infer_size` (default 640); the CenterFace fallback uses `face_infer_shape` (default `(640, 360)`). All rescale boxes to native pixels, bounding per-frame cost on 4K sources.
- **Detection backend + speed.** The plate YOLO forward is the blur stage's bottleneck (seconds per 4K frame single-thread, far slower than the face detector). `_plate_session_options` therefore runs the plate session multi-threaded by default (`intra_threads=0` = all cores; `1` restores the bit-reproducible single-thread mode), and `_plate_providers` prefers `CUDAExecutionProvider` whenever the `onnxruntime-gpu` build exposes it (an order of magnitude faster per tile than CPU), always falling back to CPU. Bit-exact reproducibility is intentionally dropped for the mask: multi-thread/GPU float reductions shift a box sub-pixel, which still covers the plate, and the sidecar caches by input hash so resume is unaffected. The CPU build never lists CUDA, so this is a no-op until the GPU runtime is installed — swap to it with `tools/enable_gpu_detection.sh` (replaces the CPU `onnxruntime` with `onnxruntime-gpu`==1.22 + CUDA-12 wheels; 1.27 is pinned down because ort-gpu 1.27 needs CUDA 13, which has no pip wheels yet). `_YoloDetector` calls `ort.preload_dlls()` so the pip-installed CUDA/cuDNN load without a manual `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`. The face YOLO model runs on the same session path (GPU when available), so faces are now GPU-accelerated too; the CenterFace fallback auto-selects providers (GPU first) and falls back to OpenCV's DNN backend when `onnx` isn't importable.
- **Tiled inference** (`blur_tile_px`, default 1280; `blur_tile_overlap`, default 0.15): the catch with whole-frame reduced-res inference is that a 4K frame crushed to 640px shrinks a distant face or plate to a few pixels — undetectable (an oncoming plate can score near zero whole-frame yet high when its region is at native scale). So `detect_regions` derives a grid from `blur_tile_px` (a 4K frame → 3×2 tiles, a 1080p frame → mostly one) and runs **both** detectors per tile, mapping boxes back to full-frame coords; overlap keeps a seam-straddling object whole in at least one tile. Duplicate detections from overlapping tiles are `_dedupe`'d by IoU (the mask unions boxes regardless, so this only keeps counts honest) — order-stable, so determinism holds. Cost scales with tile count (a 4K frame = 6× the inferences); `blur_tile_px ≥ frame` disables tiling (one tile, the legacy path). The YOLO face model — unlike the CenterFace it replaced — does **not** fire on foliage or reflective surfaces (it does not fire on a hood, a convex traffic mirror, roadside foliage, or a stone wall), so face precision no longer pins the threshold and tiling can chase distant faces safely at the shipping `blur_conf` (0.4). The plate model, by contrast, fires *confidently* on rectangular road signs, so it needs the aspect gate below; conf alone can't separate sign from plate.
- **Plate aspect band** (`blur_plate_min_aspect`, default 2.0; `blur_plate_max_aspect`, default 6.0): the YOLO plate model misfires at high confidence on BOTH squarish signage and over-elongated horizontal structures, and `conf` separates neither. A squarish road sign boxes near 1.5:1 at moderate confidence; a far-bank shoreline / bridge-railing band boxes at a very elongated ratio and also outscores conf. A real EU plate is long but bounded — typically about 2.4-3:1 (the physical 520×110 mm ratio bounds it head-on). So `_YoloDetector.detect` keeps only boxes inside `[min_aspect, max_aspect]` (computed on the model's pre-clamp box; faces pass `min_aspect=max_aspect=0`, a no-op for them): below 2.0 drops sign FPs (which would otherwise blur the place names viewers want to read), above 6.0 drops the shoreline/railing band, while every real plate (the full trajectory of an oncoming car) stays covered. `blur_sample_fps` sampling, the `blur_box_dilation_px` backstop plus the tracker's speed-scaled motion margin, and optical-flow tracking between samples bridge any dropped close frame.
- **Plate max-area gate** (`blur_plate_max_area_frac`, default 0.006): a third plate-FP class the aspect band can't see — the model fires on large stone walls / road verges / a bridge abutment at a *plausible* ≈2.0-2.7:1, boxing a large share of the frame, AND on large roadside **place-name signs** (at a plausible aspect, squarely in the aspect band's gap), while a real plate is a small fraction of a 4K frame (**≤0.4%** even for the closest oncoming car). So `_detect_frame` drops any plate box over `blur_plate_max_area_frac` of the FULL frame after tiles are merged (where the frame area is known); size is the separable axis here. Faces are never area-gated. **0.6%** keeps real plates (≤0.4%) while dropping signs; the residual risk is a real plate from a car passing far closer than any seen, which an operator can relax via the knob.
