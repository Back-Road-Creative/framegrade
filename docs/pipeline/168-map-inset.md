## Route-only map inset (implementation)

- **What it is** (`gomoveshift/pipeline/map_inset.py`): an opt-in RGBA overlay clip for a corner of a driving video, showing the recorded route and a position marker over the imagery already in an offline Route Story bundle. Call it "imagery with route", never a street map: there are no road vectors and no network calls. This module adds no CLI flag and no pipeline wiring; a later `--map-inset route` flag is its caller.
- **Inputs.** A bundle directory; route `segments` whose `time_s` is already on the source clock that the `EditTimeline` maps to (the caller aligns the GPS clock); an `EditTimeline`; the output frame size; `inset_fraction` (default 0.20 of the frame width, 4:3); `corner` (`bottom-left` default, any of four) with `margin_fraction` (default 0.02 of the width); `fps` (default 10); optional `RouteTrim` and `PrivacyZone`s; `min_coverage` (default 0.5).
- **Privacy first.** `trim_route` then `mask_zones` run before the extent, labels or any pixel is computed; nothing is drawn for a start or end pin. The framing tries margins 12%, 6%, 2%, 0% of the kept route's box and takes the first whose extent holds no point the trim removed. If even 0% holds one (a loop that returns to its start), the clip is refused. Points removed by a zone are left out of the extent, the line and the marker, but an interior zone sits inside the route's box by construction, so only trim-removed points are guaranteed outside the extent. A label inside a zone, or within 250 m of a trim-removed point, is not drawn.
- **Framing.** One fixed north-up extent per clip, sized so a pixel covers the same ground east-west and north-south (longitude span scaled by the cosine of the centre latitude). The finest bundle raster (`bundle_opening_rasters`) that fully covers the extent is used; none covering is a refusal, never a partial composite. **Base image, rendered once.** Imagery, route line with halo (`route_halo_width_px`, `route_line_width_px` at the inset width), place labels from the bundle's `labels.geojson`, and the raster's credit text in a strip along the bottom (wrapped, never truncated). The base is cached on the `InsetPlan`; each frame copies it and draws the marker.
- **Marker per frame.** `output_to_source(i / fps)` decides: no position is an insert hold, two positions a crossfade, both hide the marker. One position gives a source time, located in the privacy-processed segments. A time between segments, inside a masked hole, or outside the route hides the marker as a gap. Nothing is interpolated across a segment boundary or a cut, and no travelled line is drawn. `marker_state(plan, t)` is the cheap no-pixel form; `render_inset_frame(plan, t)` is the pure one-frame renderer.
- **Refusals** (`MapInsetRefused`, a `ValueError` listing every reason, raised before any rendering or write): bundle missing or any `verify_bundle` problem; route empty after privacy; the framing cannot exclude trim-removed points; imagery not covering the extent; marker-visible fraction below `min_coverage`; a timeline with no duration. Never an empty or partial clip.
- **Output.** `render_route_inset(...)` pipes frames to ffmpeg as a QuickTime `.mov` (`qtrle`, `argb`) at the inset size and fps, and writes `<clip>.receipt.json` (`map-inset-receipt/1`: bundle manifest sha256, the privacy `receipt_fragment`, frame size, inset rect `x, y, w, h` in output pixels, fps, frame count, hidden frames by reason `insert`/`crossfade`/`gap`, credits, wall time). The receipt holds no route coordinates and no local paths. Both files are written to a `.part` name and renamed on success. **Tests:** `tests/test_map_inset.py` (the ffmpeg-backed case skips without ffmpeg and ffprobe).
