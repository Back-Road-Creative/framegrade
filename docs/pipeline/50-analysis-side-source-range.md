## Analysis-side source range (`gomoveshift/analysis/frame_range.py`)

Raw GoPro footage is full-range YUV and carries no range tag, and every automatic YUV→RGB (or →gray) conversion assumes LIMITED on an untagged stream. Anything reading those pixels as numbers — a histogram, an absolute HSV bound, an edge count — is then reading a domain the footage never used. The grade chain states the range with `setrange=<probed>` ahead of its first filter; this module is that same decision for the readers that feed analysis rather than pixels, and a sweep of every such reader is what wired the last of them.

`probe_color_range` answers `"full"` or `"limited"` from a cached `ffprobe` of `stream=color_range`, mapping ffprobe's several spellings of each and defaulting an untagged, `unknown`, or unreadable stream to `"full"` — the same vocabulary and default the encode side uses, so a reader and a writer never disagree about what an untagged file means. Two consumers hang off it, one per shape the readers come in. **ffmpeg commands** take `setrange_filter(path)` at the head of the chain: motion's two gray decoders and the sign pass's single-frame PNG grab. For motion that is consistency rather than rescue (frame differencing largely self-cancels a uniform shift), but an untagged reader is one filter edit away from mattering; for the sign grab it is load-bearing, since `SIGN_COLORS` are absolute HSV bounds read straight off the converted frame. **`cv2.VideoCapture`** exposes no range control at all, so `align_cv2_frame_range` corrects afterwards (`v * 219/255 + 16`, the exact gain a full-range tag applies at the conversion) in `SignDetector.detect_candidates` and `ShortsExtractor.analyze_segment_visual_interest` — the latter feeding the saturation/contrast/edge scores that decide which footage becomes a short. That form cannot recover a value clipped at 0 or 255, which is why new code should prefer the ffmpeg one. Both forms are no-ops on a limited-range source, and `tests/test_analysis_range_tagging.py` pins them to the same numbers on a full-range one. That agreement is the point of the sweep: a half-tagged codebase is worse than an untagged one, because then two readers disagree and nothing says which is right.
